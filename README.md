#HADES: A Parameterized Tool for Autom[fa1.1]ated Life Cycle Assessment Modeling of Hydrothermal Carbonization and Anaerobic Digestion Systems

Abstract
Integrated hydrothermal carbonization and anaerobic digestion systems offer significant potential for the valorization of wet biomass. However, the conditions under which HTC–AD integration is environmentally justified, and the configuration through which these benefits are maximized, remain poorly understood. This lack of understanding limits informed decision-making and has kept HTC–AD integration largely theoretical rather than practically implementable. Critically, no existing framework couples mechanistic process modelling with life cycle assessment in a way that propagates feedstock variability and operating conditions through to environmental outcomes across both integration sequences. This study presents HADES (the Hydrothermal–Anaerobic Digestion Environmental Study tool), an open-source tool that addresses this gap by linking a machine learning model for hydrochar property prediction, an extended anaerobic digestion model (PW-ADM1) with inhibition kinetics specific to HTC process water, and a Brightway2-based LCA engine within a single executable workflow. Two integration pathways are evaluated: HTC followed by AD on process water (Pathway A), and AD followed by HTC on digestate (Pathway B). A case study on food waste treatment demonstrates that feedstock composition and HTC operating conditions are primary drivers of environmental performance, directly governing hydrochar and biomethane yields and the magnitude of substitution credits. For human health, Pathway A consistently outperforms Pathway B across all scenarios, while ecosystem quality shows a pathway-dependent trade-off under certain conditions. A sensitivity analysis confirms that HADES outputs are robust to internal PW-ADM1 parameterization uncertainty, with model assumption perturbations producing marginal changes in both endpoint categories. HADES is released as an open-source tool with a Streamlit interface, making mechanistic HTC–AD LCA accessible to both modelers and practitioners.
